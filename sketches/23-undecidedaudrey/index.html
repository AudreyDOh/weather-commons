Two asks at Daegwallyeong

When people ask the sky for weather, in a rite or with an aircraft, and the weather comes, who made it happen? Eight cases from seven countries, and one at length: Daegwallyeong, Korea, in the drought of 2025.

23 · Audrey Doh · started 2026-11-02 · research in progress

In brief. Weather cannot be run twice, so no one can prove that a rite or a seeding flight brought the rain.

What happened. In the Gangneung drought of August 2025, a rain rite was held at the Daegwallyeong shrine on 23 August and cloud seeding aircraft flew from 26 August. Heavy rain came three weeks later. The meteorologists said themselves that the rain on their flight days did not come from seeding.

What the numbers show. In late August, a day of 50 mm or more follows within three weeks about half the time, rite or no rite. To tell a 10 % seeding effect from chance would take about 2,750 seeded and 2,750 unseeded rainy days; there have been 1,426 such days in this season since 1940. Seeding is tried when clouds are already raining, and rites are held when a drought is near its end: both act when the sky is already about to change.

The approach. The sky happens once, so a cause is never observed; it is assembled from choices. The page asks whether the rain was unusual under 72 reasonable definitions of “rain after the act”: it followed the rite and the flights in half of them, and was ordinary in almost all. It also shows how the weather is measured, and how credit is claimed or declined in Daegwallyeong, Dubai and San Diego.

Numbers from ERA5 reanalysis for one 25 km cell, and from news reports; see Sources and limits.

Dubai, UAE · 16 April 2024

The ask. The UAE seeds clouds as a routine national programme, and its president calls rain prayers at mosques on set days.

What followed. About 254 mm fell at Khatm Al Shakla, Al Ain, in under 24 hours, the most since records began in 1949. Dubai flooded.

Who claimed what. Seeding was blamed. The National Center of Meteorology said it did not seed during the event; a spokesman later told Bloomberg it had seeded on 14 and 15 April, before the storm peaked. World Weather Attribution found storms like this 10–40 % heavier in today’s climate. Bloomberg also reported that a university statement ruling out seeding did not mention the university’s funding from the programme.

What cannot be known. Whether seeding in the days before added anything to a storm of that size.

Mandalika, Indonesia · 20 March 2022

The ask. To keep rain off the MotoGP race, a rain handler (pawang hujan), Rara Istiani Wulandari, performed a ritual on the track. The national research agency BRIN, the weather agency BMKG and the air force also ran weather modification for 18–20 March.

What followed. Heavy rain delayed the race by about an hour, then stopped.

Who claimed what. BRIN described its techniques as making rain fall before it reached the circuit. BMKG’s deputy for meteorology said the forecast had the rain ending around 16:15 local time, and that its stopping after the ritual was coincidence.

What cannot be known. Which of the three to credit: the ritual, the aircraft or the forecast.

Al Jazeera · Bisnis.com · GridOto

Bhopal, India · 19 July and 11 September 2019

The ask. In a dry spell, a local group, Om Shiv Seva Shakti Mandal, married two clay frogs to please Indra, the rain god. Eight weeks later the same group divorced them.

What followed. The organisers said rain came the next day. By September Bhopal had 81 % more rain than normal, according to an India Meteorological Department official, and the state was flooding.

Who claimed what. The same rite was credited with bringing the rain, then asked to stop it.

What cannot be known. How the monsoon would have gone with no wedding and no divorce.

Onmanorama · The Logical Indian · ETV Bharat

Utah, USA · 4–6 June 2021

The ask. With most of the state in extreme drought, Governor Spencer Cox invited Utahns of all faiths to pray for rain over a weekend. Utah also pays for cloud seeding in winter, through its Division of Water Resources.

What followed. An opinion column reported light showers in the Salt Lake Valley that weekend and called them coincidence. We found no measured account. In 2023, after a record snowpack, the governor called a day of prayer and thanksgiving, and the legislature put $12 million one-off and $5 million a year into seeding.

Who claimed what. Critics asked for water policy instead of prayer. In 2023 the governor gave thanks in prayer for the snow, in the same year the state expanded seeding to make more of it.

What cannot be known. How much of any winter’s snow was prayed for, seeded or simply weather.

Beijing, China · 8 August 2008

The ask. 1,104 rain-dispersal rockets fired from 21 sites, from late afternoon to 23:39, to keep rain off the Olympic opening ceremony.

What followed. The ceremony stayed dry.

Who claimed what. The Beijing Meteorological Bureau said the rockets intercepted a rain belt heading for the stadium. Guinness World Records lists it as the first rain dispersal for an Olympics, and notes a reported 41 % chance of rain that evening.

What cannot be known. The evening without rockets. With a 41 % chance of rain, a dry night was already the likelier outcome.

Guinness World Records · Irish Times

Lahore, Pakistan · 16 December 2023

The ask. Aircraft sent by the UAE seeded clouds over Lahore, with at least 48 flares, to wash smog out of the air.

What followed. The chief minister said about 10 % of the city got rain. The next day the US air quality index fell from 318 to 189, still “unhealthy”.

Who claimed what. Officials called it a success despite limited rain and poor clouds. The chief minister also said smog usually eases after five to seven days. The Pakistan Air Quality Initiative questioned the cost.

What cannot be known. What the air would have done that week anyway.

Dawn · Arab News · Aaj News

San Diego, USA · January 1916

The ask. The city agreed, by word of mouth, to pay the rainmaker Charles Hatfield $10,000 if he filled the Morena reservoir.

What followed. Days of heavy rain. On 27 January the Lower Otay Dam failed. Accounts of the dead range from about 14 to more than 20.

Who claimed what. The city called the rain an act of God and did not pay; Hatfield sued, and is said to have called it an act of Hatfield. Accounts of the court rulings conflict.

What cannot be known. What his chemicals did. His formula died with him.

Daegwallyeong, Korea · August–September 2025

In August 2025 Gangneung, on the coast below Daegwallyeong, ran short of water. Its main reservoir fell below a fifth of its capacity, and the city restricted supply. Within a few days of each other, two kinds of request went up to the same sky.

    Daily ERA5 rainfall, August and September 2025.

    Reanalysed: bars are daily ERA5 precipitation for one cell about 25 km across that contains the pass and Gangneung. It is not the rain measured in Gangneung or over its reservoir. From news reports: the rite (◇) and the flights (▲) are placed by the dates the reports give. The reports name only three of the six flight days.

    The Korea Meteorological Administration said itself that the rain on its flight days did not come from the seeding: it flew on days when rain was already falling. One of its forecasters said the effect on the drought would be “extremely minimal”.

    The same pass has a longer history of asking. In 2006 the administration’s research institute burned silver iodide at Daegwallyeong on nine easterly, foggy days to make more snow (Lee et al. 2009), and flew seeding aircraft over the range from 2008 to 2010.

    Interpretive: setting the rite and the seeding side by side is this sketch’s framing. It makes no claim that either brought rain.

    Situate

    Table: how often rain comes anyway in late August

    To say a rite or a flight made it rain, you would need to know how often rain comes anyway. The table takes every day from 16 to 30 August in the 85 years from 1940 to 2024, when there was no rite, and counts how often rain followed. It does the same for the driest 15 % of those days, as a stand-in for drought.

    Reanalysed: ERA5 daily precipitation for the same 25 km cell. Modelled: the window of days and the drought threshold are choices made for this page.

    Rain followed the 2025 rite, as it follows most late-August days. The table cannot tell the rite’s rain apart from the rain that comes anyway, and neither could the meteorologists for their flights. Seeding is only tried when the clouds are already there, and rites are held when a drought has gone on long enough to be near its end.

    Narrate

    The sky happens once. A cause is not observed; it is assembled from choices.

    There is no second 23 August 2025 without the rite, and no second 1 September without the aircraft. So every claim that an act brought rain is a claim about a day that never happened. The question here is narrower: was the rain that followed unusual?

    The Galton sky

    Each falling drop is a real day: a day between 16 August and 8 September, in one of the years 1940–2024, over the same pass. It bounces off that day’s real wind and lands at the rain that actually followed, the largest day of rain in the next three weeks. The pile is what the sky does when nobody asks.

    Then drop a 2025 day: the rite or the first flight. The faint drop beside it is where it would have landed if the ask had changed the rain by 10 %. The gap between them is the ask’s share; the width of the pile is how much the sky varies anyway. When the share is far smaller than the width, no one can see the ask’s effect: not the scientist, not the shaman.

    Seventy-two skies

    Climate scientists ask how much more likely an act made an outcome: FAR = 1 − P0 ÷ P1. That needs two numbers. One, P1, the chance of rain with the act, can never be observed: that day happened only once. The other, P0, the chance of rain anyway, changes with every reasonable choice we make. So this page leaves P1 out and asks only what can be asked: was the rain that followed unusual?

    It asks it 72 times. “Rain after the act” can mean a day of 1, 10, 30 or 50 mm; within 3, 7 or 21 days; compared with any day of the season, with drought days, or with days as dry as 2025; in the years 1940–2024 or in the current climate normal, 1991–2020. Each combination is one sky.

    Seventy-two definitions of rain after the act.

    ● rain followed the act in that sky · ○ it did not · the grid shows the choices behind each dot; point at a dot to read it.     The same act succeeds in half the skies and fails in the other half, depending only on what we call rain. Where the rite “succeeded”, that rain follows comparable days anyway at least 38 % of the time. After the flight, two skies make the rain look rare: the strictest ones, which compare with drought days and ask for 50 mm. Pick those and seeding seems to have worked; pick any of the other 70 and it does not. What changes from dot to dot is not the sky but us.

    Why it cannot be settled: success, measurement, chance and politics

    What would count as success?

    For seeding to have worked, a chain has to hold from the aircraft to the ground. Each link is measured with different instruments, and each can break.

Public claims since then, such as effectiveness “increasing more than 20 % from 2020 to 2023” (Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, 2024), do not say how it was measured.

    How is the weather measured?

    Much of the evidence is radar. A radar does not measure rain. It measures how much of its pulse comes back, the reflectivity Z, and converts it to a rain rate R with a rule of the form Z = a Rb. The numbers a and b assume how big the drops are, and differ between rain and snow and between storms. So “+10 dBZ after seeding” means different amounts of rain depending on the rule chosen.

    ·

    Modelled: textbook relations (Marshall and Palmer 1948; the US NEXRAD default, Fulton et al. 1998; Gunn and Marshall 1958 for snow). Radar beams over Daegwallyeong are also partly blocked by the mountains, and the instruments see different sizes of the world: a gauge is one point, a radar pixel is roughly a kilometre, the ERA5 cell on this page is 25 km, and the particles on the map travel about 20 km in three hours.

    Is it a coincidence?

    To tell a real effect from luck, you compare many seeded days with many unseeded ones, chosen at random. How many depends on how much rain varies from day to day and how big the effect is:

    n = 2 (zα + zβ)2 (cv ÷ e)2

    n: seeded days needed, and as many unseeded ones · zα = 1.645: a 5 % chance of crediting luck · zβ = 0.842: an 80 % chance of finding a real effect · cv: how much rain varies, standard deviation ÷ mean · e: the effect, as a share of the mean.

    Where do politics come in?

    Korean seeding has been flown for snow at Daegwallyeong before the 2018 Winter Olympics, for fine dust, for drought and for wildfires, with 7.3 billion won budgeted in 2024 and commercial use as the goal for 2028. In a crisis, an aircraft is something visible to do. Credit is claimed in budgets and press releases, and declined when rain is already falling or when a flood follows. The rite has its own politics: who holds it, who it speaks for, what it asks of the city.

    Interpretive: this reading is the sketch’s, drawn from the sources listed below.

    Older and elsewhere: in the Samguk yusa, Hwanung comes down to Taebaeksan with the Earl of Wind, the Master of Rain and the Master of Clouds (풍백·우사·운사); which mountain the myth means is debated. How to Kill a Cloud (Tuija Halttunen, 2021) follows the scientist Hannele Korhonen, funded by the UAE to make rain, as she weighs what her results can show.

    In all eight cases the weather answered, or did not, and someone took the credit or refused it. At Daegwallyeong, in the same weeks, a rite and a flight asked for rain, and rain came three weeks later, as it does most years. The meteorologists declined to credit their aircraft; the record does not say what the preservation society concluded. Neither can be tested against a day that did not happen. What can be shown is how often the sky answers anyway, and how many answers it would take to tell.
